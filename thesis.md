## 1. Sector and stage
Software-first climate and sustainability companies at pre-seed to seed: tools that measure, reduce,
price or manage emissions and energy use. Visible-to-this-engine slice (public GitHub and Hacker News signals):
- Carbon and emissions measurement and accounting (including supply-chain / Scope 3)
- Energy and grid software (forecasting, storage and EV-charging orchestration, demand response)
- Climate data and risk (physical-risk models, satellite/remote-sensing for land, methane, forests)
- Carbon markets infrastructure (credit verification and registries)

Out of scope for this engine: hardware-heavy climate tech (batteries, green steel, direct air capture).
These rarely leave public software traces, so this engine cannot see them. Stated as a limit, not hidden.

## 2. Why now
- Mandatory climate disclosure is spreading (e.g. EU CSRD, India's BRSR for large listed firms), which turns
  emissions data from optional reporting into a compliance cost with a budget.
- Electrification (EVs, heat pumps, solar plus storage) is creating many distributed assets that need software to coordinate.
- Cheaper open climate/satellite datasets and capable AI models let small teams build what once needed large data teams.

## 3. Five signals that make a company interesting
1. A named buyer with a budget line (compliance, procurement, utilities), not "everyone who cares about climate".
2. Active repo or product with commits in the last 90 days, plus growth in stars/users, not just a one-off spike.
3. A real data edge: proprietary or hard-to-get data, or a measurement method others can't copy in a weekend.
4. Evidence of use by real customers or pilots (named integrations, case studies, issues from real users).
5. Founder with relevant background (energy, climate science, sustainability compliance, or deep infra) shown publicly.

## 4. Five things that disqualify
1. Consultancy or agency selling hours, or an offsets "marketplace" with no verification method.
2. Pure content, newsletter, awareness or directory sites with no product.
3. Thin wrapper over an LLM or public API with no data, workflow or distribution edge.
4. Student project, tutorial, course material or hackathon demo with no sign of continuing.
5. Unverifiable or vague impact claims ("saves the planet") with no measurement method.

## 5. "Under the radar"
No funding announcement found in news or Crunchbase's free view at the date checked, and fewer than
~20 independent news mentions. Always worded as "no recorded funding at the time checked", never as
proof that the market missed them.

## 6. What 5 out of 5 looks like
A small team with a working product that measures or reduces emissions or energy use for a named buyer
type that faces a compliance or cost deadline, with a data or method edge, steady growth in usage over
several months, a founder with credible domain background, and no recorded institutional funding yet.
